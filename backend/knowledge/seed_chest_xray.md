## Atelectasis
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Partial collapse or incomplete expansion of lung tissue, seen as increased density, often with volume loss.
Matters: May relate to airway blockage, a recent operation or pleural disease.
Next: Clinical correlation; follow-up imaging is usually considered if it persists.

## Consolidation
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Airspace filled with fluid, pus or other material, appearing as a dense region that may show air bronchograms.
Matters: Commonly associated with pneumonia but not specific to it.
Next: Correlate with symptoms and laboratory results.

## Infiltration
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Non-specific term for increased lung density suggesting material within lung tissue.
Matters: The cause depends heavily on clinical context.
Next: Clinical correlation; compare with earlier images if available.

## Pneumothorax
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Air in the pleural space, seen as a visible pleural line with no lung markings beyond it.
Matters: May need urgent clinical assessment, depending on size and symptoms.
Next: Prompt clinical review of the patient and the image.

## Edema
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Fluid in the lungs, often shown as hazy opacities on both sides, septal lines or vascular congestion.
Matters: Often related to heart failure or fluid overload.
Next: Clinical correlation, including cardiac assessment.

## Emphysema
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Over-inflated lungs with flattened diaphragms and reduced lung markings.
Matters: Associated with chronic obstructive lung disease.
Next: Correlate with history and lung-function testing.

## Fibrosis
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Linear or net-like opacities reflecting scarring, often at the lung bases.
Matters: May suggest chronic interstitial lung disease.
Next: Further evaluation such as CT may be considered.

## Effusion
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Fluid in the pleural space, seen as blunting of the costophrenic angle or a dense layer along the chest wall.
Matters: Causes vary widely, from heart failure to infection.
Next: Clinical correlation; ultrasound or further imaging may be considered.

## Pneumonia
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Lung infection, often appearing as consolidation or patchy opacity.
Matters: Diagnosis depends on symptoms and tests; imaging alone is not conclusive.
Next: Correlate with clinical findings and laboratory results.

## Pleural Thickening
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Thickening of the pleural lining, seen along the chest wall.
Matters: Can follow infection or inflammation.
Next: Persistent or progressive thickening may need further evaluation.

## Cardiomegaly
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Enlarged heart shadow; on a standard PA film a cardiothoracic ratio above 0.5 is commonly used as a sign.
Matters: May relate to cardiac disease, though projection and breathing can exaggerate size.
Next: Clinical correlation; cardiac evaluation may be considered.

## Nodule
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Small rounded opacity in the lung, commonly under 3 cm.
Matters: Needs comparison with earlier images and risk-factor assessment.
Next: CT follow-up is often considered according to size and risk factors.

## Mass
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Larger rounded or irregular lung opacity, commonly over 3 cm.
Matters: Warrants prompt further evaluation.
Next: CT and specialist review are usually considered.

## Enlarged Cardiomediastinum
Source: Prototype seed note (unverified, replace with a cited guideline)
What: Widened heart or mediastinal outline.
Matters: Causes range from technical factors such as rotation or an AP film to vascular or other disease.
